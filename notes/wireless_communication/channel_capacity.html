A note on communication channel

The image of the big pipe is intentional. The communication channel is nothing but a big invisible pipe throught the thin air. There is certain limitation on how much stuff one can funnel through the pipe and similarly, there is a certain amount of 'frequency content' one can circulate through the communication channel! In this short note, I summarize a coherent and tractable account on the theoretical grounds on which the gigantic world of communication and information transfer is revolving. There are several interesting and beautiful concepts in this realm including entropy, mutual information, capacity, bandwidth, channel use that are essential building blocks of a science that we know as the 'information theory'.

Before anything, we need to first determine how we can quantify information. Claud Shanon and hist collegue Fanou, dedicating themself to the very same cause at the MIT, developed a concept based on entropy to create a mathematical framework to quantify the information. Considering the fact that information roots in 'uncertainty', it is natuaral to quantify the amount of information based on its uncertainty. Building upon the same notion, Shanon defined the entropy for the event \(x\) in terms of its probability \(p\) as \[H(x) = -p \log_2(p)\quad \text{bits} \]
