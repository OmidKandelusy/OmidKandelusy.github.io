How wireless communication works?

I often find myself in situations that I am being asked on how a wireless communication system works, and as a PhD holder in this very field (though being focused on the systematic aspects), the expectation is that I should be able to explain this in a very simple way. As Einstein famously said, if one cannot explain something in a simple way, it basically implies a lack of true understanding on the subject matter. Hence, I decided to make this casual piece of writing to explain this with some plain words and some rigorous expressions from the thick book of electromagnetic, that every ECE student should pass in the undergrad education. Anyways, how this magical wonder of this universe, conveying some knowledge from point A to point B without any tangible and visually distinguishable thread of connection, works?!

Let's think loud about an experiment with static charges like the balloons and a piece woolen cloth. Let's move a bit deeper and translate that into the rules of electro static physics. As we know, any static charge would create an electric field around itself which in turn will exert force any other charge in its vicinity. So, we can establish a very rudimentary wireless communication set up where a piece of conductor attached to a force meter can detect a yes message and a no message as follows:
  • force detected: yes ⇒ or in digital word a logic high (1)
  • no force detected: no ⇒ or logic low (0)
Such an on-off switching for information transfer, directly translates into an electromagnetic wave because the Maxwell's equations prove that any change in the electric field generates magnetic field and vice versa. Hence, the wireless communication though electric signals is in essence only possible through the electromagnetic waves. To better envision this, a viable wireless information transfer would entail very fast on-off switching which evidently result in a grand a coexistence show of electric and magnetic components. However, similar to virtually everything, once the science establishes a fact, its on poor engineers to deliver something that runs on the theorized and abstract concept discovered by the scientists! There are a quite few challenges that we engineers should overcome before we can have a practically meaningful and efficient wireless system. One of the big challenges and perhaps the biggest is how to direct an electromagnetic wave from the point A in such a way that a far located receiver at the point B could detect it. There are fantastic nuances involved in this engineering task. To appraoch it, we first need to know how electromagnetic waves propagate in the physical word.

According to Maxwell's law, the direction of propagation for an electromagnetic wave in the free-space (no electric charge present)must be always perpendicular to both of the electric and magnetic components of the wave. Mathematically speaking,this can be described as the cross-product of the electric and magnetic components, which is called the Poynting vector. Thus, we want our alternating electric signal at the touching point with the air or free space to have a strong Poyting vector, i,e large electric and magnetic components. To achieve this goal, we should find a physical configuration for the touching point which maximizes the alternating signal level. Long story short, we cannot hook up any thing to an alternating signal for the radiation; instead, we need a proper geometry of conductors which we call "Antenna". Well, it was Marconi who used this term to describe the touching points in his set up, the thing was indeed a vertical pole or "l'antenna" in Italian language!

However, this is only half of the story. The touching point in the receiver side also determines how efficient we can translate the striking waves into electrical signals. Well, kind of the same math applies! Specifically, the electric signal that the wave generate on the touching point of the receiver follows the same equation with the same conclusion: the geometry of the touching point governs the shape and form of the generated signal. Hence, for an effective communication we need proper antennas at both ends. However, that's still not all the whole story yet. On the receiver side, two additional things should be considered as well. First, what mechanism we want to use for generating the electrical signal from the striking waves ? Particularly, the electromagnetic wave has two components and both can be used for this goal. However, it is more convenient to use the electric field component because from the Maxwell's law, voltage is line integral of electric field component and a surface integral of the magnetic component. In other words, we only need a a metal rode to generate electircal signals out of the electric component of the electromagnetic waves whereas relying on the magnetic field would need a loop. As can be guessed, the former is clearly more convinient.

The second important thing is to make sure that the striking wave's electric component hits the receiver's conductors as much as possible. For example, If the electric field amplitude is alternating horizontally, the receiver antenna should be placed horizontally, otherwise the generated voltage out of the integrator would be next to nothing! This hit and miss situation is technically explained using the 'polarization' concept. The polarization of an electromagnetic wave is the contour that the tip of electric field component creates while alternating in time. Hence, we can have vertical, horizontal, circular, elliptical, etc polarized electromagnetic waves that would entail their own specific antennas to establish an efficient communication.
